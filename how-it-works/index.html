How it works

One parcel record for the whole deal team.

A development decision needs the developer, the land use attorney, the broker, and the lender to agree on the same facts. Land to Yield builds that record for a City of Los Angeles parcel: what the site is, which rules reach it, what it could yield, and what is still unproven.

What the record contains

Parcel facts

APN, lot area, zoning string, overlays, current use, and assessed value, each linked to the official system it came from and the date it was pulled.

Rule path

Base zoning, the SB 79 tier and distance band, the City's status for the parcel, and the Low-Rise menu where it applies, with the citation for each step.

Yield and economics

Units, floor area, height, and parking under each path, carried into a pro forma where every assumption is visible and editable.

The workflow

1

Identify the parcel

Enter an address or APN and confirm the exact parcel. A nearby address or a point on a map does not establish eligibility.

2

Resolve the rules

Read current zoning and overlays, then the parcel's status under SB 79 and Ordinances 188967 and 188968: available, temporary hold, excluded, or Low-Rise.

3

Test the yield

Compare base zoning, the statutory envelope, and the Low-Rise menu side by side, then run preliminary economics and sensitivities.

4

Hand off the open items

Every unresolved fact stays on a list with the source to check, so the attorney, architect, and lender start from the same page.

What still needs a professional

  • Exact parcel identity, legal description, ownership, and any assemblage assumptions.
  • The current zone string, general plan designation, overlays, specific plans, and adopted maps on the relevant date.
  • Whether a state or local housing pathway actually applies to the parcel and the proposed project.
  • Tenant protections, replacement units, demolition limits, affordability, environmental review, subdivision, and procedure.
  • Whether the program and design can use the screened envelope, and whether the deal can be financed on it.
Product boundary: Land to Yield is a preliminary screen. It is not legal advice, a zoning determination, an appraisal, a test fit, or a commitment to lend, and a result should not be quoted as a final developability conclusion.
Coverage: Parcel screening currently covers the City of Los Angeles. Countywide or statewide coverage should not be assumed unless a tool states it.
