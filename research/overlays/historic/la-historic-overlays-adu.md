## 1. LA City Historic Preservation Overlay Zones (HPOZs)

An HPOZ is a zoning overlay tool codified in **LAMC Section 12.20.3** that protects neighborhoods composed of architecturally and historically significant structures. Any new project within an HPOZ must complement the neighborhood's historic character.

## 2. How HPOZs Impact ADU Design

### The Core Tension

California state ADU law (Gov. Code 65852.2) grants property owners the **right** to build compliant ADUs in residential zones. HPOZ rules can influence **how the ADU looks and where it sits** on the lot, but they **cannot effectively prohibit** an ADU that otherwise meets state law.

### Contributing vs. Non-Contributing Properties

Each HPOZ's Historic Resource Survey classifies every property:

| Classification | Definition | ADU Impact |
|---------------|------------|------------|
| **Contributing** | Original or largely intact historic structure | Strictest design review; must preserve character-defining features |
| **Contributing Altered** | Historic structure with some modifications | Moderate review; alterations should not worsen existing changes |
| **Non-Contributing** | Newer or heavily altered structure | More design flexibility; must still be compatible with district |

### Design Requirements for ADUs in HPOZs

- **Massing & Scale:** Match dominant building heights in the district; step back upper stories to reduce visual impact
- **Materials:** Use "compatible, not identical" exterior materials — similar siding types, roof materials, and window styles that align with the district era
- **Visibility:** Prioritize rear-yard placement; keep rooflines positioned to avoid dominating the streetscape
- **Primary Structure Protection:** Preserve original rooflines, porches, windows, and architectural details; avoid enclosing historic porches or cutting new openings in prominent facades
- **Roof Forms:** Match or complement the prevailing roof type (gable, hip, flat) in the district
- **Window/Door Proportions:** Maintain proportions consistent with the historic period

### Best Strategies for HPOZ ADU Approval

1. **Garage conversions** are often the easiest path — the massing and location already exist, with fewer visual changes on the street-facing side
2. **Detached rear-yard ADUs** placed far from the street frontage minimize visibility concerns
3. **Single-story ADUs** face less scrutiny than two-story structures
4. **Simple, vernacular designs** are typically favored over overtly modern or overly ornate designs

## 3. Secretary of the Interior Standards Compliance

The **Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation** are the nationally recognized framework for preservation work. If your ADU project follows these standards, it will satisfy HPOZ requirements.

### The 10 Standards (Summary for ADU Context)

1. Use the property for its historic purpose or a compatible new use
2. Retain the historic character — do not destroy distinctive materials or features
3. Recognize each property as a physical record of its time; do not create a false sense of history
4. Preserve changes that have acquired historic significance in their own right
5. Treat distinctive features with sensitivity
6. Repair rather than replace deteriorated features; match originals when replacement is necessary
7. Avoid chemical or physical treatments that damage historic materials
8. Protect and preserve archaeological resources
9. **New additions shall be compatible but differentiated** — they should not destroy historic materials and should be reversible
10. **New construction shall be undertaken so that, if removed in the future, the essential form and integrity of the historic property would be unimpaired**

### Practical Application to ADUs

Standards 9 and 10 are most relevant to ADU construction:

```
KEY PRINCIPLE: A new ADU should be COMPATIBLE with the historic district
character but clearly DIFFERENTIATED as new construction. It should be
REVERSIBLE — meaning if removed, the historic property remains intact.
```

- An ADU should not replicate a historic style so closely that it creates a false impression of age
- Materials and proportions should harmonize with the district without being a pastiche
- The ADU should be subordinate to the primary historic structure in scale and prominence

> **Source:** [National Park Service — Secretary of the Interior's Standards](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1739/secretary-standards-treatment-historic-properties.htm)

## 4. HPOZ Review Process for ADUs

### Review Categories

Depending on the scope of work and the property's classification, your ADU project will fall into one of four review categories:

| Review Type | Abbreviation | When It Applies |
|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Certificate of Appropriateness** | COA | Major alterations or new construction on contributing properties |
| **Certificate of Compatibility** | CCMP | New construction on non-contributing properties |
| **Conforming Work — Contributing** | CWC | Minor work on contributing structures |
| **Conforming Work — Non-Contributing** | CWNC | Minor work on non-contributing structures |

### Step-by-Step Process

```
Step 1: Research & Pre-Consultation
  - Verify HPOZ status and property classification via ZIMAS
  - Review the specific Preservation Plan for your HPOZ
  - Consult with LA City Planning staff (pre-application meeting recommended)

Step 2: Concept Design
  - Design must meet BOTH state ADU standards AND HPOZ guidelines
  - Focus on placement, massing, materials, and compatibility
  - Minimize changes to character-defining features of primary structure

Step 3: Formal HPOZ Review
  - Minor work: Staff-level approval (faster)
  - New construction / significant additions: HPOZ Board review (public hearing)
  - Submit: site plans, elevations, material specifications, contextual photos

Step 4: Building Permits
  - After HPOZ clearance, proceed to standard building permit review
  - Standard plan check and construction inspections apply
```

### Timeline Expectations

HPOZ review adds **4-12 weeks** to the standard ADU permitting timeline depending on whether staff-level or board-level review is required. Budget extra time and submit complete drawings to avoid delays.

> **Source:** [LA City Planning — Historic District Project Review](https://planning.lacity.gov/preservation-design/historic-district-project-review)

## 5. Mills Act Implications for ADU Additions

### What Is the Mills Act?

The **Mills Act Historical Property Contract Program** is the City of Los Angeles's most significant financial incentive for historic preservation. It is a **revolving 10-year contract** between the City and the owner of a qualifying historic property that provides potential **property tax reductions of 40-60%** in exchange for using savings to rehabilitate and maintain the property.

### Qualifying Properties

- **Historic-Cultural Monuments (HCMs)** designated by the City
- **Contributing structures** within HPOZs

### How ADU Construction Affects Mills Act Tax Benefits

```
CRITICAL: When new construction (like an ADU) is added to a Mills Act
property, the market value of the new construction is ADDED to the
restricted value of the historic property.

  Mills Act Assessed Value = Restricted historic value + Market value of new ADU

The ADU portion does NOT benefit from the Mills Act tax reduction.
Only the historic structure itself retains the reduced assessment.
```

### Key Considerations

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Tax on ADU** | ADU assessed at full market value; no Mills Act discount |
| **Contract compliance** | ADU must not violate Mills Act maintenance obligations |
| **Design standards** | ADU must comply with Secretary of Interior Standards to maintain Mills Act eligibility |
| **Contract risk** | Poorly designed ADU that damages historic character could jeopardize the Mills Act contract |
| **Rental income** | ADU rental income may affect the Income Approach valuation used for Mills Act assessment |

### LA County Mills Act Program

LA County also operates a Mills Act program for properties in unincorporated areas, administered through the Department of Regional Planning.

> **Sources:**
> - [LA City Planning — Mills Act Program](https://planning.lacity.gov/preservation-design/historic-resources/incentives-resources/mills-act)
> - [LA County Planning — Mills Act Program](https://planning.lacounty.gov/long-range-planning/mills-act-program/)

## 6. National Register of Historic Places Districts in LA

The **National Register of Historic Places (NRHP)** is the federal government's official list of properties worthy of preservation.

### LA County Overview

- **618+** properties and districts listed on the National Register in LA County
- **21** National Historic Landmarks
- **250+** listings within the City of Los Angeles (including 11 National Historic Landmarks)
- **130** listings in Pasadena (including 5 National Historic Landmarks)
- **202** listings elsewhere in the county (including 5 National Historic Landmarks)

### Notable National Register Historic Districts in LA

| District | Location | Significance |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| Hollywood Boulevard Commercial & Entertainment District | Hollywood | Entertainment history |
| Broadway Theater & Commercial District | Downtown LA | Theater architecture |
| Angelino Heights | Echo Park | Victorian residential |
| Hancock Park | Mid-Wilshire | Early 20th century residential |
| Country Club Park | Mid-City | Period Revival residential |
| Baldwin Hills Village | Baldwin Hills | Garden-city planning |
| Highland Park-Garvanza | Northeast LA | Craftsman architecture |
| Whitley Heights | Hollywood | Mediterranean Revival |

### ADU Impact of NRHP Listing

- NRHP listing alone does **not** impose local design review requirements
- However, many NRHP districts **overlap** with local HPOZs, which do impose review
- Properties on the NRHP are automatically listed on the **California Register of Historical Resources**
- State ADU law (Gov. Code 65852.2) allows local agencies to impose standards that "prevent adverse impacts on any real property that is listed in the California Register of Historical Resources"

## 7. California Register of Historical Resources

The **California Register of Historical Resources (CRHR)** is the state's authoritative guide to significant architectural, archaeological, and historical resources.

### Automatic Inclusions

The following are automatically listed on the California Register:
- All California properties on the **National Register of Historic Places**
- All **California Historical Landmarks** (No. 770 and above)
- All **California Points of Historical Interest** (evaluated and recommended)

### Relevance to ADU Law

California Government Code Section 65852.2 specifically references the California Register:

```
Gov. Code § 65852.2(a)(1)(A):

Local agencies may impose standards on ADUs that include, but are not
limited to:
  - Parking
  - Height
  - Setback
  - Landscape
  - Architectural review
  - Maximum size of a unit
  - Standards that prevent adverse impacts on any real property that
    is listed in the California Register of Historical Resources
```

### Important Distinction

The statute references **only** the California Register of Historical Resources — it does **not** explicitly reference local registers or purely local historic designations. However, most locally designated historic properties (HCMs, HPOZ contributing structures) are also eligible for or listed on the California Register through SurveyLA documentation.

### What "Prevent Adverse Impacts" Means in Practice

Local agencies can require:
- Architectural review for exterior design compatibility
- Height and setback restrictions beyond standard ADU minimums
- Material specifications consistent with historic context
- Placement requirements (e.g., rear-yard only)

Local agencies **cannot**:
- Use historic designation to effectively prohibit ADUs entirely
- Impose subjective design standards that function as a veto
- Require minimum lot sizes for ADUs

> **Source:** [Cal. Gov. Code § 65852.2 — CA Legislative Information](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=65852.2)

## 8. SB 9 Prohibition in Historic Districts

### Original SB 9 (2021) — Historic District Exclusion

When SB 9 (California HOME Act) was enacted in 2021, it included a broad exclusion for historic properties:

```
Gov. Code § 65852.21(a) — Original Exclusion:

SB 9 two-unit developments were DISQUALIFIED on parcels that are:
  (1) Listed in the State Historic Resources Inventory
  (2) Within a historic district (state or local)
  (3) Designated as a landmark

This effectively BLOCKED SB 9 for ALL parcels in any HPOZ,
regardless of whether the specific parcel had historic value.
```

### AB 1061 (2025) — Narrowing the Exclusion

**AB 1061**, signed by the Governor on October 10, 2025 (effective January 1, 2026), significantly **narrows** the historic exclusion:

#### What AB 1061 Changed

**Government Code § 65852.21 (Two-Unit Developments):**
A parcel is disqualified from SB 9 ministerial approval **only if**:
1. The parcel is **individually listed** in the State Historic Resources Inventory, OR
2. The parcel is a **landmark property**, OR
3. The parcel **contains a contributing structure** within a State Historic Resources Inventory historic district or local historic district

**Government Code § 66411.7 (Urban Lot Splits):**
Excludes projects only if they involve:
1. State Historic Resources Inventory landmark properties
2. Local landmark sites
3. Demolition or alteration of contributing structures in California Register or local historic districts

#### What This Means in Practice

```
BEFORE AB 1061 (pre-2026):
  Parcel in ANY HPOZ → SB 9 BLOCKED (even non-contributing properties)

AFTER AB 1061 (effective Jan 1, 2026):
  Non-contributing parcel in HPOZ → SB 9 ALLOWED
  Contributing parcel in HPOZ → SB 9 BLOCKED (unless no alteration
    to contributing structure)
  Vacant parcel in HPOZ → SB 9 ALLOWED (no contributing structure
    to protect)
```

#### Local Agency Authority Under AB 1061

Local governments may still adopt **objective** zoning, subdivision, and design review standards to maintain historical value. These standards must:
- Regulate **measurable factors** (height, setbacks, exterior materials, lot size)
- Use "measurable or observable" criteria
- **Not** require subjective judgment

## 9. ADU Bills: What Applies in Historic Areas

### Summary Matrix

| Law | Effective | Applies in Historic Areas? | Notes |
|-----|-----------|---------------------------|-------|
| **Gov. Code 65852.2** (State ADU Law) | Ongoing | **YES** — ADUs cannot be prohibited | Local agencies may impose design standards to prevent adverse impacts on California Register properties |
| **AB 68 (2019)** | Jan 2020 | **YES** | Streamlined ADU approvals; historic design review still permitted |
| **AB 881 (2019)** | Jan 2020 | **YES** | Eliminated many ADU barriers; historic standards preserved |
| **SB 13 (2019)** | Jan 2020 | **YES** | Reduced fees; no historic exemption from fee reductions |
| **AB 2221 (2022)** | Jan 2023 | **YES** | Clarified ADU standards; local agencies retain historic review authority |
| **SB 897 (2022)** | Jan 2023 | **YES** | Height increases for ADUs; historic areas may impose lower heights if justified |
| **AB 1033 (2023)** | Jan 2024 | **YES** | ADU condo conversion; HPOZ design review still applies |
| **SB 9 (2021)** (Duplexes/Lot Splits) | Jan 2022 | **PARTIALLY** — see AB 1061 below | Originally excluded all historic districts |
| **AB 1061 (2025)** | Jan 2026 | **EXPANDED** SB 9 into historic areas | Non-contributing parcels now eligible for SB 9 |
| **SB 9 (2025)** (New bill, same number) | Jan 2026 | **YES** | Ensures local ADU laws comply with state law; nullifies illegal local barriers |

### Key Principle

```
STATE ADU LAW PREEMPTION:

California state law REQUIRES cities to allow ADUs. A historic overlay
can regulate the DESIGN of an ADU but CANNOT be used to effectively
PROHIBIT an ADU.

However, SB 9 (lot splits and duplexes) has specific historic district
carve-outs — these are separate from ADU rights under Gov. Code 65852.2.

ADU ≠ SB 9. They are different programs with different historic rules.
```

### What Historic Districts CAN Regulate for ADUs

- Exterior materials and finishes
- Roof form, pitch, and materials
- Window and door proportions and placement
- Building height (within state ADU height limits)
- Setbacks (within state ADU setback limits)
- Site placement and orientation
- Landscaping and hardscape

### What Historic Districts CANNOT Do

- Prohibit ADUs outright
- Impose minimum lot sizes for ADUs
- Require owner-occupancy (unless otherwise allowed by state law)
- Charge excessive fees beyond standard rates
- Deny ADUs based purely on subjective aesthetic judgment with no basis in adopted guidelines

> **Sources:**
> - [CA HCD — ADU Handbook](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/policy-and-research/adu-handbook-update.pdf)
> - [Holland & Knight — California's 2026 Housing Laws](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/californias-2026-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)

## 10. LA County Historic Landmarks & ADU Permits

### LA County Register of Landmarks and Historic Districts

The **Los Angeles County Register of Landmarks and Historic Districts** is maintained by the **Historical Landmarks and Records Commission (HLRC)** under County Historic Preservation Ordinance No. 2015-0033.

- Applies to **unincorporated** areas of LA County
- As of 2026, the County has designated **14 landmarks**
- Properties must be **50 years of age or older** and satisfy designation criteria

### Impact on ADU Permits in Unincorporated LA County

| Property Status | ADU Permitted? | Additional Requirements |
|----------------|---------------|----------------------|
| County-designated landmark | Yes | Design review by County Planning; must protect historic features |
| Within County historic district | Yes | Compliance with district design guidelines |
| Adjacent to landmark | Yes | May need to demonstrate compatibility |
| No historic designation | Yes | Standard ADU permitting only |

## 11. How to Check if a Property Is in a Historic Overlay

### Step-by-Step Verification Process

#### For LA City Properties

**1. ZIMAS (Zoning Information and Map Access System)**

```
URL: https://zimas.lacity.org

Steps:
  1. Enter the property address, APN, or click on the map
  2. Look at the "General Information" tab → "Special Notes" section
  3. Check "Planning & Zoning" tab → look for "HPOZ" under Overlays
  4. Check "Historic Preservation Review" subsection
  5. Note the property classification: Contributing, Contributing
     Altered, or Non-Contributing
```

**2. HistoricPlacesLA (SurveyLA Database)**

```
URL: https://hpla.lacity.org

Steps:
  1. Search by address or browse the map
  2. Review the SurveyLA findings for the property and its context
  3. Check if the property was identified as:
     - Individually eligible for historic designation
     - A contributor to a potential historic district
     - Not eligible / non-contributing
  4. SurveyLA data feeds into ZIMAS but may contain additional context
```

**3. LA City Planning — Office of Historic Resources**

```
URL: https://planning.lacity.gov/preservation-design

For questions about:
  - Historic-Cultural Monument (HCM) status
  - HPOZ boundaries and contributing status
  - Pending historic designations
  - SurveyLA survey results
```

#### For LA County (Unincorporated) Properties

**4. LA County GIS Portal**

```
URL: https://egis-lacounty.hub.arcgis.com/datasets/lacounty::historic-resources

Steps:
  1. Search or browse the map for the property
  2. Check if the parcel intersects any designated landmarks or districts
  3. Cross-reference with HLRC registry
```

**5. HLRC Landmark Registry**

```
URL: https://hlrc.lacounty.gov/landmark-registration/los-angeles-county-landmark-registration/

Steps:
  1. Review the list of 14 designated County landmarks
  2. Check if the property is within or adjacent to any designation
```

#### For All Properties (State/Federal Registers)

**6. California Office of Historic Preservation**

```
URL: https://ohp.parks.ca.gov

Tools:
  - Built Environment Resource Directory (BERD): searchable database
    of all recorded historic resources in California
  - California Register listings
  - National Register listings for California
```

**7. National Register of Historic Places**

```
URL: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/database-research.htm

Search the NRHP database to determine if a property or district is
listed on the National Register.
```

### Quick-Check Decision Tree

```
Is the property in LA City?
  │
  ├─ YES → Check ZIMAS for HPOZ overlay
  │         ├─ HPOZ found → Note contributing status
  │         │                ├─ Contributing → HPOZ Board review required for ADU
  │         │                └─ Non-Contributing → Lighter review; SB 9 eligible (2026+)
  │         └─ No HPOZ → Check for HCM or SurveyLA findings
  │                       ├─ HCM → Design review required
  │                       └─ None → Standard ADU permitting
  │
  └─ NO → Is it in unincorporated LA County?
           ├─ YES → Check HLRC registry and County GIS
           └─ NO → Check the specific city's historic preservation program
```
