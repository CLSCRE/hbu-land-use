# Federal Regulations Affecting Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Development

*Last updated: 2026-03-11*

This document summarizes key federal statutes, agency guidance, and regulatory frameworks that affect the planning, construction, financing, and operation of Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) across the United States. While ADU permitting is primarily governed by state and local jurisdictions, several federal laws impose requirements or create incentives that developers, lenders, and property owners must consider.

---

## 1. Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. Section 3601 et seq.)

### Overview

The Fair Housing Act (FHA), enacted as Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968 and amended in 1988, prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. ADUs, as dwelling units offered for rent or sale, are subject to these protections.

### Key Implications for ADUs

- **Non-Discriminatory Tenant Selection.** Property owners renting an ADU may not refuse tenancy, set different terms, or advertise preferences based on any protected class. The exemption for owner-occupied buildings with four or fewer units (42 U.S.C. Section 3603(b)) does **not** exempt the owner from the prohibition on discriminatory advertising (42 U.S.C. Section 3604(c)).

- **Reasonable Accommodations and Modifications.** Under Sections 3604(f)(3)(A) and (B), landlords must permit reasonable modifications to an ADU at the tenant's expense and make reasonable accommodations in rules or policies when necessary for a person with a disability to use the dwelling.

- **Design and Construction Requirements.** ADUs in buildings with four or more units first occupied after March 13, 1991, must meet the seven design and construction requirements of 42 U.S.C. Section 3604(f)(3)(C), including accessible common areas, accessible routes, and adaptable interiors. Single-family detached ADUs are generally exempt from these design mandates, but local building codes may impose similar requirements.

- **Zoning and Land Use.** Municipalities may not use zoning to exclude ADUs in a manner that has a discriminatory effect on protected classes. HUD and DOJ have pursued disparate-impact claims under the Fair Housing Act against jurisdictions whose land-use policies disproportionately exclude families with children, persons with disabilities, or racial minorities.

## 2. Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and Section 504

### Overview

The Americans with Disabilities Act (42 U.S.C. Section 12101 et seq.) and Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 U.S.C. Section 794) impose accessibility requirements on certain categories of housing. Their applicability to ADUs depends on the funding source and entity involved.

### Key Implications for ADUs

- **Privately Funded ADUs on Single-Family Lots.** The ADA generally does not apply to privately owned residential properties that are not places of public accommodation. A homeowner building an ADU on their own lot with private funds is not subject to ADA Title III requirements.

- **Publicly Funded or Subsidized ADUs.** If ADU construction receives federal financial assistance (e.g., CDBG funds, HOME Investment Partnership grants, HUD Section 8 project-based vouchers), Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act requires that a minimum of 5% of the units (but not fewer than one) be accessible to persons with mobility impairments, and an additional 2% be accessible to persons with hearing or vision impairments. See 24 CFR Section 8.22.

- **State and Local Government ADU Programs.** If a state or local government operates an ADU incentive program (e.g., pre-approved ADU plans, public land leasing for ADU construction), ADA Title II requires that the program be administered in a non-discriminatory manner and that resulting units comply with applicable accessibility standards.

- **UFAS and ADA Standards.** Where accessibility is required, units must comply with the Uniform Federal Accessibility Standards (UFAS) or the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, depending on the funding source and applicable regulation.

## 3. FHA Lending Guidelines: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac ADU Appraisal Policies

### Overview

The federal government, through its Government-Sponsored Enterprises (GSEs) Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, sets guidelines that determine whether and how properties with ADUs can be financed with conforming loans. These policies directly affect the marketability and appraised value of ADU properties.

### Fannie Mae (FNMA) ADU Policy

- **Selling Guide Section B2-3-04 and B4-1.3-05.** Fannie Mae permits ADUs on properties securing single-family mortgages. An ADU is defined as an additional living area that is subordinate in size to the primary dwelling and includes a separate entrance, kitchen, and bathroom.

- **Appraisal Treatment.** As of late 2023, Fannie Mae updated its policies to allow ADU rental income to be considered in qualifying borrowers for purchase and refinance transactions. The ADU may contribute to the appraised value of the property using a comparable-sales or income approach, provided the appraiser identifies appropriate comparable properties.

- **Key Restrictions.** The property must be a one-unit property with an ADU (not classified as a two-unit property). The ADU must be legally permitted under local zoning. Unpermitted ADUs may be excluded from the appraised value.

### Freddie Mac ADU Policy

- **Single-Family Seller/Servicer Guide, Section 5601.11.** Freddie Mac similarly recognizes ADUs on one-unit properties. Freddie Mac allows ADU rental income to be used for borrower qualification under specific documentation requirements.

- **Appraisal Requirements.** The appraiser must identify the ADU and determine whether it contributes to or detracts from value. The ADU must have independent exterior access and meet local permitting requirements.

### FHA (Government-Insured Loans)

- **HUD Handbook 4000.1 (FHA Single Family Housing Policy Handbook).** FHA-insured loans may be used for properties with ADUs, provided the ADU does not cause the property to be classified as a two-unit property and complies with local zoning. FHA appraisers must address the ADU in the appraisal report and determine its contribution to value.

- **FHA 203(k) Rehabilitation Loans.** Borrowers may use FHA 203(k) loans to finance ADU construction or conversion as part of a rehabilitation project on an eligible property.

## 4. IRS Tax Implications for ADU Owners

### Overview

The Internal Revenue Code imposes specific tax obligations and provides certain benefits for property owners who construct and rent ADUs. The primary areas of concern are rental income reporting, depreciation, expense deductions, and exchange treatment.

### Rental Income (26 U.S.C. Section 61; IRC Section 212)

- ADU rental income is reportable as ordinary income on Schedule E (Form 1040). All rents received must be reported regardless of amount.
- Deductible expenses include mortgage interest (allocated to the ADU portion), property taxes (allocated), insurance, repairs, utilities, and property management fees attributable to the ADU.

### Depreciation (26 U.S.C. Section 167, 168)

- **Residential Rental Property.** An ADU used for rental purposes is depreciable over **27.5 years** using the straight-line method under the Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System (MACRS). See IRC Section 168(c) and (b)(3).
- **Depreciable Basis.** The depreciable basis includes construction costs, permits, architectural fees, and other capitalized costs. Land is never depreciable. If the ADU is built on an existing parcel, the owner must allocate basis between the land and the improvement.
- **Bonus Depreciation.** Under Section 168(k), bonus depreciation applies to certain qualified improvement property but generally **does not** apply to residential rental property itself. However, certain personal property placed in the ADU (appliances, furnishings) may qualify.

### Section 1031 Like-Kind Exchanges

- Under 26 U.S.C. Section 1031, as amended by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, like-kind exchanges are limited to **real property**. An ADU held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment may qualify for a Section 1031 exchange if replaced with like-kind real property.
- **Mixed-Use Considerations.** If the primary residence includes an ADU used for rental, only the ADU portion may qualify for Section 1031 treatment. The owner-occupied portion is not eligible (it may instead qualify for the Section 121 exclusion on sale of a primary residence, subject to its own requirements).

### Section 121 Primary Residence Exclusion

- Under 26 U.S.C. Section 121, a taxpayer may exclude up to $250,000 ($500,000 for married filing jointly) of gain on the sale of a principal residence. If the property includes a rental ADU, the IRS may require the gain to be allocated between the owner-occupied portion and the rental portion. The rental portion is not eligible for the Section 121 exclusion and is subject to capital gains tax (and potential depreciation recapture under Section 1250).

## 5. HUD Stance on ADUs as Affordable Housing Strategy

### Overview

The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) has increasingly recognized ADUs as a viable strategy for expanding affordable housing supply, particularly in high-cost markets. While HUD does not directly regulate ADU construction, it influences ADU policy through funding programs, research, and technical assistance.

### Key HUD Positions and Programs

- **HUD Policy Development and Research (PD&R).** HUD's Office of PD&R has published research supporting ADUs as a tool for increasing housing supply without significant infrastructure costs. ADUs are highlighted as a strategy for aging in place, intergenerational housing, and gentle densification of single-family neighborhoods.

- **Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Program.** Local governments may use CDBG funds (authorized under Title I of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974, 42 U.S.C. Section 5301 et seq.) to finance ADU construction for low- and moderate-income households. CDBG-funded ADUs must meet Section 504 accessibility requirements and other federal cross-cutting requirements (environmental review, Davis-Bacon prevailing wages if applicable, lead-based paint disclosure).

- **HOME Investment Partnerships Program.** HOME funds (42 U.S.C. Section 12741 et seq.) may be used for ADU construction or rehabilitation as part of affordable housing strategies. HOME-funded units are subject to rent limits and income targeting requirements for the applicable affordability period.

- **Housing Choice Voucher Program (Section 8).** Tenants holding Housing Choice Vouchers may use them to rent ADUs, provided the unit meets Housing Quality Standards (HQS) and the rent is reasonable. This expands the potential tenant pool for ADU owners and supports the integration of affordable housing into established neighborhoods.

- **Biden-Harris Administration Housing Supply Action Plan (2022) and Subsequent Guidance.** The federal government has encouraged state and local governments to reform zoning to permit ADUs as part of broader housing supply initiatives. Executive actions and interagency coordination have positioned ADU-friendly zoning reform as a condition or priority in certain competitive federal grant programs.

## 6. FEMA Flood Zone Requirements for ADU Construction

### Overview

The National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), administered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) under the National Flood Insurance Act of 1968 (42 U.S.C. Section 4001 et seq.), establishes minimum floodplain management standards that communities must adopt and enforce as a condition of participation in the NFIP. ADUs constructed in Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs) must comply with these standards.

### Key Requirements for ADUs in Flood Zones

- **Special Flood Hazard Areas (Zone A, AE, AH, AO, V, VE).** Any new construction, including ADUs, in an SFHA must comply with the community's floodplain management ordinance, which must meet or exceed the minimum NFIP requirements at 44 CFR Section 60.3.

- **Elevation Requirements.** In Zone A and AE, the lowest floor of a new residential structure (including an ADU) must be elevated to or above the Base Flood Elevation (BFE). In Zone V and VE (coastal high-hazard areas), the bottom of the lowest horizontal structural member must be at or above the BFE, and the structure must be elevated on pilings or columns. See 44 CFR Section 60.3(c)(2) and (e)(4).

- **Substantial Improvement Threshold.** If ADU construction constitutes a "substantial improvement" to the existing property (defined as any reconstruction, rehabilitation, addition, or other improvement with a cost equaling or exceeding 50% of the market value of the structure before the improvement), the entire structure must be brought into compliance with current floodplain management requirements. See 44 CFR Section 59.1.

- **Flood Insurance Requirements.** Properties with federally backed mortgages located in SFHAs must maintain flood insurance under the Flood Disaster Protection Act of 1973 (42 U.S.C. Section 4012a). Adding an ADU may affect insurance premiums, particularly under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0 methodology, which considers building characteristics, replacement cost, and flood risk.

- **Detached ADUs.** A detached ADU in an SFHA is treated as new construction and must independently meet all elevation, anchoring, and floodproofing requirements. It must have its own elevation certificate.

- **Executive Order 11988 (Floodplain Management).** Federal agencies and federally funded projects must avoid construction in floodplains where practicable. ADU projects receiving CDBG, HOME, or other federal funds in a floodplain must undergo a floodplain management review under 24 CFR Part 55.
