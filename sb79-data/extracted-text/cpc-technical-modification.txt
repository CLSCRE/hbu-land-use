ADDITIONAL INFORMATION IN RESPONSE TO PUBLIC COMMENT FOR CASE NO.
CPC-2026-1797-CA

The Department of City Planning has prepared additional information for the City Planning
Commission's (CPC) consideration to incorporate information related to public comment
received after the release of the draft ordinances, as well as provide additional analysis
regarding the Low-Rise Incentive Area program incentives and performance standards
recommended in the Low-Rise Ordinance.

Summary of Public Comment on Low-Rise Incentive Area Incentive Relief and Performance
Standards

Since the release of the ordinances associated with the City of Los Angeles' local
implementation approach, the Department has received and reviewed numerous comments
submitted in response to the incentives and performance standards contained in the Low-Rise
(formerly Corridor Transition) Incentive Area program (referred to as "Low-Rise"). In particular,
some commenters have encouraged the CPC to consider amending the Low-Rise Ordinance to
enforce a 15 foot rear yard setback in the Low-Rise program citing concerns related to safety,
impacts to Los Angeles' urban canopy, and adverse effects to existing private open space.
Commenters also called for restoration of the rear yard common open space option to
encourage flexibility in the configuration of Low-Rise program projects and improve overall
project feasibility. Finally, commenters recommended consideration of a front yard
encroachment allowance for balconies to support the design and construction of private open
space in a manner consistent with the goal to cultivate active and pedestrian-oriented streets.

Additional Options for Consideration

City Planning has developed several considerations for the CPC to evaluate based on the
feedback received. These proposed options offer setback, open space, and development
standard allowances to address the concerns described above. These options have been
analyzed for consistency with SB's 79 statutory requirements and are consistent with building
volume allowances required for local implementation.

Yard Setbacks
Within the Low Rise (formerly Corridor Transition) program, incentives and performance
standards have been calibrated to create low-rise missing middle outcomes found in the historic
architecture of Los Angeles (courtyard apartments, bungalow courts, townhomes, etc).
Setbacks, parking standards, and open space requirements have all been designed to integrate
contextual density and produce modulated low-scale developments. The Low-Rise Ordinance
proposes 15-foot front yard or prevailing, which ever is less, and maintains the existing
programs setback standards of a 4-foot side yard for three or more story structures, a 3-foot
side yard for two or less story structures, and a 4-foot rear setback provided structures maintain
a height of less than 26-feet within 15-feet of the rear lot line.

In response to public comment, the Department analyzed the feasibility of maintaining
compliance with SB 79, while requiring 15 foot front yards, 5 foot side yards, and 15 foot rear
yards within the Low Rise program. This analysis found that on a 5,000 or 7,500 square-foot lot,
with the additional development standards included in the ordinance, these yard requirements
would not allow for the development of half of the SB 79-allowed building volume within 200 feet
of a Tier 1 Transit Oriented Development (TOD) Stop, a ¼ mile of a Tier 1 TOD Stop, 200 feet of
a Tier 2 TOD Stop, and ½ mile of a Tier 2 TOD Stop (as required by Government Code Section
65912.161(b)(1)(A) for phased implementation) and, therefore, would not meet SB 79's
requirements.

The introduction of a 15-foot rear yard setback with the proposed 15-foot front yard setback
would require additional height relief in order to offset the resulting limited developable area.
Both the increase in setbacks and height would result in a taller, more narrow development
typology, which may not comport with the low-scale goal the program was designed to address.
Furthermore with regard to comments voicing concerns related to access and fire standards, all
projects are reviewed for compliance with applicable building code requirements during the
permitting process. This process includes review by the Department of Building and Safety and
the Los Angeles Fire Department to ensure compliance with any access or setback requirement
in the applicable State Minimum Fire Safety Regulations and/or Fire Code which may result in
higher building separation or hardening requirements dependent on the project location and
conditions.

Staff further analyzed alternative options for an increased rear setback and found that an 8 foot
rear yard setback without a required building stepback would comply with SB 79 minimums. As
such, to permit rear-yard flexibility to support enhanced tree canopy and open space, while
maintaining existing front yard requirements, the Commission could explore requiring an 8-foot
rear setback without a transitional height standard.

Open Space
To promote street-oriented open space and building modulation on the primary facade, the
Department previously eliminated options for rear yard open space. However, in response to
public feedback requesting private outdoor areas, rear yard open space allowances may be

Page 4

reintroduced in conjunction with front yard permissions. Restoring the rear yard open space
incentive could encourage the creation of rear-facing open areas by providing specific open
space credits within the rear yard setback.

Balcony Encroachments
To support greener corridors for residents and pedestrians alike, the Department maintains its
recommendation in the Low-Rise Ordinance to enforce a front yard setback that equates to 15
feet or the average of a site's adjacent lots, whichever is less. In response to comments, CPC
could consider allowing a balcony front yard encroachment of up to five feet to promote
community-oriented outdoor space and design versatility.
