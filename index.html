The rule and the reply

The State wrote the rule. Los Angeles wrote the reply.

SB 79 sets statewide development floors around qualifying transit. Los Angeles used the statute's own alternative and exclusion provisions to phase it in and to offer a smaller-scale path in its lower-density neighborhoods. A parcel's answer depends on both.

The reply · City of Los Angeles

Two ordinances, effective June 30, 2026

Adopted under § 65912.161, which lets a city substitute an alternative plan and seek temporary exclusions, subject to state review.

No. 188968

Phased Implementation Ordinance

Sequences where the statute applies. Most station-area parcels sit on temporary hold under the statute's exemption pathways, with phasing running to around 2030.

  • Temporary pathways include existing local capacity, low-resource areas, fire hazard, sea level rise, and historic resources
  • Some stations cannot be delayed and apply the statute now
No. 188967

Low-Rise Ordinance

A local menu for residential neighborhoods near transit: small multi-unit buildings instead of the statutory envelope.

  • LR-1: 5 to 11 units · LR-2: 12 to 16 units
  • FAR from 1.30 to 2.90, two to three stories
  • No parking minimum · restricted affordable units required

Questions

Common questions.

Is a Land to Yield result a legal conclusion?

No. It is a preliminary screen built from public parcel, zoning, planning, and legislative sources, with the unresolved verification steps listed. Confirm the current ZIMAS record, controlling maps, overlays, and legal pathway with qualified professionals before relying on any result.

How does it handle LA's response to SB 79?

Parcel status comes first. A parcel on temporary hold or excluded under the City's lists does not receive a statutory envelope from proximity alone. Where the Low-Rise Ordinance may apply, the screen calculates the LR-1 or LR-2 menu and flags that the subarea must be confirmed in ZIMAS.

What areas does it cover?

The core workflow is designed for parcels within the City of Los Angeles. Source availability varies by parcel and tool. Broader LA County and Southern California coverage is being evaluated.

How is this different from ZIMAS or CoStar?

ZIMAS provides official parcel-linked planning information, and CoStar focuses on market data. Land to Yield organizes those kinds of inputs into pathway screens, transparent scenarios, and a verification list. Qualified professionals still determine what can legally and physically be built.

Can I export reports for clients or investors?

Yes. PDF one-pagers, LOI drafts, and pitch decks export from any analysis.
