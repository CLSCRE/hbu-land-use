Eligible by-right

The Statute

What SB 79 actually does.

SB 79 — the Abundant and Affordable Homes Near Transit Act, Cal. Gov. Code §65912.155–162 — declares housing a permitted use on any residential-, mixed-, or commercial-zoned site within ½ mile of a qualifying transit stop. Local zoning cannot override SB 79's density, height, and FAR standards. Review is ministerial: no CEQA, no Planning Commission, no Council.

Who passed it, and when does it take effect?
The California Legislature enacted SB 79 (Wiener) in 2025. It takes effect statewide on July 1, 2026. Local jurisdictions including LA have authority to publish implementation models that classify parcels for permanent exclusion or temporary exemption.
What's the difference from SB 35, SB 9, AB 2011, SB 423?
SB 35 (2017) provides ministerial review when a jurisdiction misses RHNA targets and requires significant affordability set-asides. SB 9 (2021) allows lot splits and duplexes on single-family parcels. AB 2011 (2022) permits affordable housing on commercial corridors with prevailing-wage triggers. SB 423 (2023) extended SB 35. SB 79 is broader than all of them: it covers any residential/mixed/commercial parcel near transit, requires no affordability set-aside (though Density Bonus stacks if desired), and is ministerial.
Does SB 79 require affordable housing?
No. SB 79's base entitlement requires no affordable set-aside. You can build 100% market-rate. However, if you stack the State Density Bonus Law (§65915) you can add up to 50% bonus units in exchange for a defined affordable set-aside (5–24% depending on income tier).
What about parking?
AB 2097 (2022) already eliminated minimum parking near major transit. Every SB 79 site is within ½ mile of qualifying transit by definition, so AB 2097 applies — zero minimum parking required. Some Tier 2 ½-mile sites have a 0.5/unit parking ratio in LA's implementation model.
What stops SB 79 from applying to my parcel?
Three things: (1) permanent exclusion — your parcel is in an Industrial Employment Hub (LAX, Van Nuys, Downtown/SE LA, Chatsworth, Pacoima, Atwater/Cypress Park) or has no walking path under one mile to the TOD stop; (2) statutory exemption — your parcel is zoned Public Facilities, Parking, or Open Space; (3) RSO demolition — your project on a site with > 2 units would require demolition of any LA Rent Stabilization Ordinance unit occupied in the last 7 years. Beyond these, eight temporary exemption pathways may apply (see the Phasing section).
How fast is approval?
Per §65912.158(b): 60 days for projects of 150 units or fewer; 90 days for larger projects. Plan-check only; no entitlement, no hearings.
